## Patterns
1. This stretch of the series moves to named real places more often than the early books: Morro Bay, Padre Island/Corpus Christi, Veracruz, Hells Canyon, Isle Royale, San Clemente Island, Fort McCoy, Milwaukee, St. Louis, Denver, the Diefenbunker and Mesa Verde.
2. Water is the main theme. Half the books turn on a coast, an island, a bay or a big river: the Gulf, the Pacific isles, Lake Superior, Lake Michigan, the Mississippi, the Ohio and the Rio Grande.
3. Cold-war military sites keep coming back as hidden vaults or tech caches: silos, chemical arsenals, SAC bases, the congressional bunker and Canada's shelters. These rows carry `bunker`.
4. The town at the heart of each book is usually invented (a vice town, a healing-waters town, a scientist's town, a swamp barony, a junction town). These rows use a real stand-in with the same shape, and the invented town is logged in `invented_skipped`.
5. The regional spread is wide: Southwest desert and plateau (NM/AZ/UT/CO), Gulf and Louisiana swamp, Great Plains and Badlands, Great Lakes, Pacific islands, Appalachia/Ozarks and Canada. The northeast and New England barely appear after #72.
